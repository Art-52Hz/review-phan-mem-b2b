## Core Features: What Actually Matters for Freelancers

### 1. Voice Customization and Emotion Controls

Most AI voice tools give you pitch and speed sliders and call it a day. Murf goes further with something that's genuinely uncommon at this price point: tone and emotion selectors on certain voices. You can push a voice toward "happy," "angry," or "terrified" depending on the script context.

For dialogue-heavy audiobooks or dramatic narration, this is a real differentiator. It removes at least one layer of post-production problem-solving. The catch — and this is worth noting — is that not every voice has emotion controls available. You'll need to test voice by voice to find which ones support it.

The platform also gives you word-level pronunciation customization. If a voice keeps mangling a brand name or a technical term, you can fix it at the individual word level and preview the corrected pronunciation before rendering the full project. That's a time-saver on client work where a mispronounced product name would be an embarrassing revision.

*Based on the research: The pronunciation fix feature was flagged in one review specifically because AI voices have a well-known problem with proper nouns and industry jargon. Murf's word-level correction is a practical solution, not just a checkbox feature.*

### 2. Multi-Character Dialogue and Script Structure

The text block splitting system lets you assign different voices to different parts of a script — sentence by sentence if needed. For audiobook producers, this is where Murf genuinely outperforms simpler tools. You can create a multi-character scene where each character has a distinct voice, different pacing, and different emotional tone, all within the same project.

The "apply voice to entire project" button also deserves a mention. On long-form scripts, the ability to reassign a voice across all blocks in one click is the kind of workflow detail that saves real time on tight deadlines.

Manual pause insertion is required, which adds friction — the AI does not detect natural pause points in your script automatically. On a 10-minute narration, manually tuning pauses across every sentence is tedious. Plan for that in your production time.

### 3. Background Music Integration and Media Import

Murf includes a stock music library that you can layer directly under your voiceover within the platform. You can also upload your own music files — you're not limited to their catalog. For quick-turnaround client projects where you'd otherwise be jumping between a voiceover tool and a separate audio editor, having this inside one interface is a genuine convenience.

There's also a direct URL import from YouTube, Vimeo, or Dailymotion if you're repurposing existing video content with a new voiceover track. That's a specific workflow that content agencies and video repurposing freelancers will find useful.

*My Take: Multiple reviewers noted that Murf voices sound noticeably better when music is layered underneath. I want to flag that as both a feature and a red flag. A good standalone voiceover should hold up without background music covering it. When music is load-bearing for making output sound professional, that tells you something about raw voice quality.*

### 4. Language Support and Accent Variety

Twenty-plus languages on the Pro plan, with multiple English accent variants (US, UK, Australian, Canadian) across different voices. For freelancers doing localization work — adapting content for different regional English-speaking markets specifically — this is a meaningful feature. The accent differentiation is not cosmetic; reviewers confirmed the regional distinctions are audible and usable.

The German accent demo that surfaced in one review showed it functioning at a reasonable quality level for international content. That said, language breadth is capped on lower tiers — the Basic plan covers 10 languages, which might be sufficient depending on your client mix.
