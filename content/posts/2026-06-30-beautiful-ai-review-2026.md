## Annual or monthly: the break-even point

The $14.50 headline is a monthly equivalent, not a monthly price. You pay $174 at once.

- One project lasting one month: monthly Pro costs **$45**, annual costs **$174**. Monthly wins by $129.
- Three separate months of work in a year: monthly costs **$135**. Still cheaper than annual.
- Four or more months of work in a year: monthly costs **$180** or more, so annual becomes cheaper.

Simple rule: if you expect to use Beautiful.ai in four or more months of the next twelve, buy annual. If you are not sure, start with one monthly payment for a real project, then decide.

For a team, run the same math per seat. If billing is per user, three people on annual cost 3 x $40 x 12 = **$1,440 per year**, while three people on monthly cost **$150 per month**. The pricing page also says "fixed pricing for up to three users" on the Team card, which may mean a lower total. Because the page uses both labels, check the full invoice amount before you enter a card.

## The 14-day free trial: read this before you start

The trial lasts 14 days and works for Pro or Team. A credit card is required. The pricing FAQ says the card "will be charged in full if you don't cancel your trial before the trial period ends."

Before you start the trial:

1. Note the exact date and time the trial ends, and set a reminder one day earlier.
2. Check which plan and billing period the trial will convert to. If you only need one project, make sure you are not converting into an annual charge.
3. Prepare a real test deck so you use the 14 days well (see the checklist below).

You can cancel in the Billing section of your Profile. After cancelling a paid plan, you keep Pro access until the end of the billing cycle.

## Refund rules are strict

Quoted from the vendor FAQ on November 3, 2026:

- Annual subscriptions "may be eligible for a partial refund if a request is submitted within 24 hours of the initial purchase or renewal."
- "Monthly subscriptions are non-refundable."
- Cancelling after payment has been processed, whether for a trial or a renewal, is charged "the full monthly rate for that billing period."
- "All refunds are subject to a processing fee."

In practice, treat every payment as final. The 24-hour window on annual plans is short, and even then the refund is partial. This is the main reason to test properly during the trial instead of after paying.

## What you get for the money

**Smart Slides.** Layouts adjust spacing and alignment as you add content, so a slide stays tidy when the text changes. Classic Slides give more manual control. Test both with your own longest heading and densest paragraph, not with the demo content.

**AI generation.** You can draft a deck from a prompt. The vendor lists it as unlimited on paid seats. Expect to rewrite the text for any client work; the value is in the starting structure, not in finished copy.

**Team features.** Team adds live collaborative editing, a shared slide and template library, custom branded themes, engagement tracking and analytics, and multi-language support. These matter only if more than one person edits decks.

**Enterprise features.** SSO, user provisioning (SCIM), audit and change logs, and stated SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, CCPA and PCI compliance. A freelancer will not need these.

## Exports: editable PowerPoint is not the same as slide images

This is where client projects usually go wrong. The vendor's [export guide](https://support.beautiful.ai/hc/en-us/articles/30629528652685-Exporting-your-slides-and-presentations), checked October 5, describes these options:

| Export choice | Documented behavior | Check before client delivery |
|---|---|---|
| Editable PowerPoint | Available on Pro, Team and Enterprise; includes editable presentation elements and notes | Open the file on the client's machine and edit representative text and objects |
| PowerPoint Images | Slides become static images; notes are included | Do not offer this as individually editable slide content |
| Google Slides | Editable export on Pro, Team and Enterprise; embedded video, audio, transitions and animations are lost | Decide whether the missing media or effects are required |

The [editable PowerPoint instructions](https://support.beautiful.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360035562031-How-do-I-export-as-an-editable-PowerPoint) also warn that missing fonts can change formatting. External exports do not keep Beautiful.ai's native transitions and animations. These are documented limitations, not results from a deck we ran ourselves. Count the cleanup time as part of the price.

## A 14-day trial checklist

[Download the blank presentation handoff checklist](/downloads/presentation-handoff-checklist.txt) and use it during the trial. It has no prefilled scores.

1. **Write down the deliverable.** Shared link, PDF or editable PowerPoint? Which fonts and brand colors? Who edits the deck after you hand it over?
2. **Build one representative deck.** An opening slide, a text-heavy slide, a chart, an image with an unusual aspect ratio, and a closing call to action. Use content you are allowed to upload; do not paste private client data just to test a feature.
3. **Export it in the client's format** and open it in the client's app. Check fonts, chart labels, line breaks and whether objects stay editable.
4. **Time your work.** Record preparation time, revision time and export cleanup. Compare with how long the same deck takes in your current tool.
5. **Decide before the trial ends.** If exports or brand treatment do not meet the client's requirements, cancel and keep your current workflow.

## Who should pay, and who should not

**Pay for annual Pro** if you are a freelancer or consultant who delivers pitch decks, reports or proposals most months, and your clients are fine with a link, a PDF, or a PowerPoint file you check after export.

**Pay for one month** ($45) if you have a single deck project with a clear deadline. Cancel after delivery; there is no refund on monthly plans, but you also avoid a $174 commitment.

**Consider Team** only when two or more people need to edit the same branded decks. Confirm the seat count and invoice first.

**Skip it** if you make one or two decks a year, if your client needs heavy animation or video in Google Slides, or if the client's in-house team will rebuild every slide in PowerPoint anyway.
