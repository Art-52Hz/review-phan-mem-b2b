## 3. Descript — Best for Podcast and Video Editors

Descript is fundamentally different from the other tools on this list — it's an audio/video editor where AI voice is one component of a broader production workflow. You record or import audio, Descript transcribes it, and you edit by editing the text. The Overdub feature lets you add or replace words using an AI clone of your voice.

For podcasters and video creators who already edit audio and want AI voice integrated into the editing workflow, Descript is uniquely efficient.

**Standout feature:** Edit audio by editing text; voice overdubbing in the same timeline

**Honest limitation:** Steeper learning curve than standalone voice tools; overkill if you just need TTS

**Price:** $24/month (Creator plan)

## 4. Play.ht — Best for High-Volume Production

Play.ht positions itself as a high-throughput TTS platform — it handles bulk text-to-speech conversion more efficiently than tools optimized for single pieces. It has one of the largest voice libraries (900+ voices, 140+ languages) and an API designed for production scale.

## 5. Speechify — Best for Personal Audio Consumption

Speechify is different from the others: it's primarily a reading assistant, not a content creation tool. It converts documents, articles, and PDFs into audio so you can listen instead of read. The AI voices are high quality, but the use case is personal productivity rather than content production.

If you want to "read" faster by listening at 1.5–2x speed, Speechify is the tool. If you're creating audio content for an audience, use one of the other tools.

**Standout feature:** Best-in-class reading speed controls; Chrome extension reads any webpage

**Price:** $139/year personal plan
