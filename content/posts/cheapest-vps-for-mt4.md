## Minimum specs: separate the terminal from the whole server

OANDA's official [MT4 system-requirements guide](https://www.oanda.com/sg-en/skills-and-insights/education/platforms-and-tools/metatrader4/system-requirements-to-check-before-installing-mt4/), checked October 29, 2026, lists a 1 GHz CPU and at least 512 MB RAM, recommending 2 GHz or higher and at least 1 GB RAM. Those are terminal guidance, not a complete Windows VPS sizing rule.

The operating system, remote desktop, updates, security tools and other applications also consume resources. A terminal that launches successfully can still run out of headroom after you add charts, indicators or an Expert Advisor. Ask your broker and EA publisher for requirements covering the actual build and dependencies you intend to use.

Use this buying checklist rather than treating the smallest plan as automatically sufficient:

- **Operating system:** get the exact image and version confirmed against broker and plug-in support.
- **Memory:** observe the whole server with your intended charts and EA active, including spare capacity for maintenance.
- **CPU:** inspect sustained load and application responsiveness; core count alone does not describe your workload.
- **Storage:** leave space for history, logs, updates and recoverable configuration copies.
- **Network:** obtain the broker's server endpoint and assess the route from the candidate data center.

My buying recommendation is to treat Basic as a demo candidate and move to Business only when memory pressure justifies it. This is a selection method, not a measured terminal-capacity claim. Avoid buying extra resources before you know which resource is limiting the application.

## How to test it yourself in 15 minutes

Run this smoke test after provisioning and installation, with a demo account ready. Downloads and setup can take longer. Keep live trading disabled while checking the environment.

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Record the ordered resources, OS image and terminal build. Verify the broker server and required plug-ins.
2. **Minutes 3–6:** Load your intended charts and EA in the demo environment. Look for missing libraries, connection failures and initialization errors in the logs.
3. **Minutes 6–9:** Inspect memory, CPU and free disk space. Capture dated screenshots with account identifiers hidden and describe the workload alongside them.
4. **Minutes 9–12:** Disconnect the remote desktop without signing out, then reconnect. Inspect whether the terminal and demo configuration remain active.
5. **Minutes 12–15:** Locate backups and the access-recovery route. Assign someone to respond when the terminal fails and schedule a separate planned restart rehearsal.

### Is the published minimum RAM enough for an EA?

### Can I pay the advertised monthly price each month?

An EA requiring those calls needs a different deployment route. Check that restriction before comparing the services on price.

### Will a VPS improve my trading results?

This purchase addresses where your software runs and who maintains it. Choose it for an identified operating need. Keep strategy evaluation, broker execution checks and financial risk decisions separate from the hosting bill.
