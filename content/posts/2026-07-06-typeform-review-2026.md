## What Is Typeform?

Typeform is an online form and survey builder built around a **conversational interface**. Rather than showing every field at once, it presents one question at a time, full-screen, with smooth transitions — the form feels like a friendly conversation instead of paperwork. That design philosophy is the entire point: people are more likely to finish a form that doesn't overwhelm them.

Beyond the look, Typeform is a serious tool with conditional logic, integrations, payment collection, and analytics. It's used for lead generation, market research, customer feedback, quizzes, job applications, and event registration — anywhere a business wants responses *and* wants to make a good impression while collecting them.

In short, Typeform trades raw efficiency for engagement, and for customer-facing forms that trade-off usually pays off in completion rates.

## Key Features

### Conversational Forms

The signature feature. One question per screen, full-width, with attractive transitions and the ability to add images and video. The format reduces cognitive load and feels modern, which is why Typeform forms typically see higher completion rates than traditional multi-field forms — especially on mobile.

### Logic Jumps

Logic Jumps create branching paths so respondents only see relevant questions based on their previous answers. A "no" to one question can skip an entire section; a product choice can route to different follow-ups. This personalization keeps forms short and relevant, further boosting completion.

### Typeform AI

Typeform AI helps you build forms from a prompt — describe what you want to ask and it drafts the questions — and can generate follow-up questions, summarize responses, and surface insights from your data. It speeds up form creation and helps make sense of open-ended answers at scale.

### Integrations

Typeform connects to the tools you already use — CRMs, email platforms, Slack, Google Sheets, Notion, HubSpot, and more, plus Zapier for everything else. Responses can flow directly into your stack so a captured lead lands in your CRM automatically.

### Analytics

Built-in analytics show completion rates, drop-off points (where people abandon), and response trends, so you can diagnose and improve underperforming forms. For research and lead gen, knowing *where* people quit is as valuable as the responses themselves — a 60% drop-off on one question tells you exactly what to fix, turning your form into something you can optimize like a landing page rather than set and forget.

### Customization & Branding

Typeform gives you real control over how forms look: custom colors, fonts, logos, background images, and even video, so a form feels like a seamless extension of your brand rather than a generic third-party widget. On paid plans you can remove Typeform's own branding entirely. You can also collect payments inside a form (via Stripe), embed forms anywhere on your site, and end with custom thank-you screens that redirect or upsell — small touches that, together, make Typeform feel less like a survey tool and more like a polished part of your funnel.

## When Typeform Is (and Isn't) Worth It

The honest way to decide on Typeform is to ask what the form is *for*. Typeform's entire value proposition is engagement — its conversational format lifts completion rates and leaves a polished brand impression. That value is real and measurable, but it only matters in certain contexts. When the form is customer-facing — a lead-capture form, a quiz lead magnet, an onboarding survey, a customer-research questionnaire — those few extra percentage points of completion and the brand polish translate directly into more leads, better data, and a stronger impression. In those cases Typeform's premium pays for itself.

When the form is purely functional and internal — an employee poll, an internal data-collection sheet, a quick RSVP — the conversational experience and branding don't move the needle, and you're paying for polish nobody will notice. There, a free tool like Google Forms or Tally does the job for nothing. So the decision isn't "is Typeform good?" (it clearly is) but "does this particular form's audience and stakes justify paying for experience?" Match the tool to the job, and reserve Typeform for the forms that are part of your customer journey.
