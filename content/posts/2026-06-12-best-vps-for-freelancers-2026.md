## Sell the client a responsibility map

Write down who owns billing, deployment, application updates, incident diagnosis and restoration. Assign each item to a named role rather than to “support.” A host's help channel can assist with its service without becoming the client's website maintainer. The client needs to know whom to contact when a business function fails.

Use that map to choose the hosting model. If the client wants a finished application with little involvement and you cannot provide ongoing operations, evaluate managed application hosting first. If the client needs custom runtimes and accepts an operating agreement, a VPS can be a more relevant shortlist.

## Handoff is part of the purchase

Decide whether the client or freelancer owns the hosting account and domain. Confirm that the client can recover access if the working relationship ends. Keep deployment instructions and a record of dependencies that another authorized operator could use; a server reachable only through one person's laptop is a fragile handoff.

Do not place passwords or tokens in a shared checklist. Record the access owner and approved recovery route instead. The goal is to make future operations possible without turning the project notes into an exposed credential store.

## Choose a recovery check before launch

A useful acceptance plan includes a safe restore, followed by a check of the client's actual application function. For a contact site that may be a form; for another workload it may be a background task or authenticated page. Define the check before migrating so both parties know what completion means.

Keep the previous working deployment until the replacement passes the agreed criteria. If the client will not fund recovery preparation, explain that operating boundary before buying a plan. Upgrading infrastructure is not a substitute for deciding who accepts the result and who handles a future incident.

## Operational details and worksheets

A freelancer should choose hosting around the client's workload and the operational responsibilities they can fulfill. Do not assume that moving to a VPS automatically improves speed, reliability or profit.

### Write down the reason for changing hosting

Name the constraint in the existing environment: a required application, observed resource limit, deployment requirement or a support responsibility you need to resolve. Record how you identified it. If the issue is still unknown, investigate it before committing to a server migration.

Use the existing site as a baseline. Keep the workload and measurement method consistent when evaluating a candidate. A vendor's resource label or promotional price cannot establish your client's result.

### Decide who operates the environment

| Responsibility | Agreement to record |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Who owns the hosting account and billing relationship? |
| Access | Who needs which permissions, and how is access removed after handoff? |
| Maintenance | Who performs updates and checks application compatibility? |
| Monitoring | Who receives alerts and responds outside project hours? |
| Recovery | Who maintains backups and demonstrates a restore? |
| Support | What is included by the host versus your own service scope? |

Resolve these responsibilities with the client before treating hosting as a minor subscription expense. Keep a written escalation path and avoid promising response coverage you cannot deliver.

### Evaluate each client site's requirements

Record software, database, storage, expected workload and required integrations. For several client sites, document resource allowances and the recovery plan for each one. A single advertised server price does not establish that every site will fit or that the arrangement suits their access requirements.

For WordPress projects, use our [compatibility and backup checklist](/posts/best-vps-for-wordpress-2026/). Verify the proposed environment against the actual application rather than applying one generic server size to all clients.

### Budget for operating work

For a current quote, check Hostinger plans and compare the full commitment with the requirements below.

Record the initial invoice, renewal, billing term, required licenses, backups, traffic charges and paid support. Mark unresolved charges unknown. Include your active maintenance and migration time with the same hourly value used for your baseline workflow.

Separate the server invoice from the portion allocated to each client. Explain the allocation basis and what happens if a client leaves or uses more resources. A lower allocated figure does not reduce the payment due now.

### Prove recovery before handoff

Restore a permitted sample in an isolated environment and check the user journeys the client needs. Record dependencies, steps and unresolved issues. Keep a rollback plan before changing production.

Deliver the agreed documentation: ownership, access, billing, maintenance, backup and escalation details. Hosting access alone is not evidence that the client can operate or recover the site.

For a WordPress client site, download the [blank handoff worksheet](/downloads/wordpress-handoff-worksheet.txt). Record account ownership, the quoted plan, recovery requirements, an authorized test result and ongoing costs. Leave untested items unknown; the worksheet is a planning aid, not proof that a provider passed a recovery test. Keep passwords, tokens and private customer data out of the shared record.

### Make a bounded decision

Keep the existing hosting arrangement as an option when it meets the requirements. Shortlist a VPS only when it resolves an identified need and the operating responsibilities are covered. Confirm the selected provider's current terms before buying; this guide does not certify a provider or promise uptime.

## FAQ

### Which VPS hosting is best for freelancers?

Hostinger is a candidate for freelancers able to operate the software environment. For a client who needs application maintenance included, compare the managed application model before choosing a server.

### How much does a freelancer VPS cost?

Use the actual hosting commitment plus licenses, backups and your operating work. A headline equivalent rate alone does not establish the full cost of a client project.

### Does a small client site need a VPS?

Only shortlist one when a concrete runtime or configuration requirement justifies it. Existing shared or static hosting may already satisfy the deliverable.

### Does the freelancer have to manage VPS software?

The Hostinger VPS overview describes a customer-managed software environment. Assign that work explicitly in the client agreement instead of assuming the infrastructure provider owns it.

### Should the client own the hosting account?

Decide ownership before purchase and document billing and recovery access. The appropriate arrangement depends on the service agreement, but the handoff must remain workable if the relationship ends.
