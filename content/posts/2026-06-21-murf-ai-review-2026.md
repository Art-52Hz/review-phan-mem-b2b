## What the free trial is (and is not) for

The trial gives you 10 minutes of voice generation and 10 minutes of transcription with no credit card, according to the [free trial guide](https://help.murf.ai/is-murf-free-to-use). The pricing page describes it as everything in the Business plan except downloads, with no commercial rights. The [trial-duration FAQ](https://help.murf.ai/how-long-does-the-free-trial-last) describes a trial limited by usage rather than a fixed end date.

That makes the trial useful for one job: deciding whether a Murf voice can read *your* script well enough. It is not a way to deliver client work. Murf's help center states that [download and export require a paid plan](https://help.murf.ai/can-i-download-my-project-during-the-free-trial), so plan the purchase before the deadline, not after the client approves a preview.

Ten minutes runs out quickly if you regenerate whole paragraphs. Test the hardest 60 to 90 seconds of the script first: names, numbers, product terms and the transition your client cares about most.

## How voice generation time is really used

Studio bills by Voice Generation Time (VGT). The [official VGT explanation](https://help.murf.ai/what-is-voice-generation-time-vgt) says generating new text or changing text uses the duration of the affected block. Your delivered file can therefore be much shorter than the VGT you used to produce it.

A simple budget example (illustrative, not a measured benchmark):

| Step | Rendered audio |
|---|---|
| First full draft of a 5-minute explainer | 5 minutes |
| Client changes the product name in 3 blocks | about 1 minute |
| Pacing fix after review, with text edits | about 1 minute |
| **VGT used for one 5-minute delivery** | **about 7 minutes** |

At that rate, Creator's 24 yearly hours cover roughly 200 finished five-minute videos a year, or about 17 a month. Busy course creators can use that up. Occasional explainer work will not come close.

Two rules help. First, the [voice-settings guidance](https://help.murf.ai/does-changing-voice-settings-sped-pitch-etc-consume-vgt) says changing speed or pitch on rendered, unchanged text does not use extra VGT. So get the script approved before you render, then adjust delivery. Second, unused VGT is not a savings account: the [carry-forward policy](https://help.murf.ai/vgt-carry-forward) limits rollover to the next billing cycle and describes losing it on cancellation.

## Commercial rights: what each plan allows

Murf's [commercial rights article](https://help.murf.ai/do-i-have-commercial-rights-over-the-voice-over-created), checked October 31, 2026, says all paid Studio plans include commercial rights over generated voiceovers, including monetized YouTube and streaming content when the content is original. The free trial does not include commercial rights.

Three details matter for freelancers:

- **Business license.** Murf recommends the business license for registered businesses. On the pricing page it starts at the Business plan, not Creator. If your client is a company that will publish under its own brand, ask which entity holds the subscription and which plan its legal team expects.
- **Broadcasting.** TV and commercial radio need broadcasting rights, which the help center reserves for Enterprise plans. The pricing table lists full broadcasting rights as an Enterprise add-on.
- **Platform rules change.** Murf warns that YouTube and other platforms update their AI-voice policies. Check the destination's current disclosure rules for each upload.

A paid plan does not give you rights to the script, music or footage. Clear those separately.

## Creator vs Business: which plan to buy

**Choose Creator if** you are a solo freelancer or educator who narrates in Canva, delivers fewer than about 15 short videos a month, and publishes under your own name or for small clients who accept a standard commercial license. Creator includes unlimited downloads, all export formats and the Canva integration.

**Choose Business if** at least one of these is true:

1. Your client is a registered business and wants the business license.
2. You build narration inside PowerPoint or Google Slides and want the plugin rather than exporting audio by hand.
3. Your scripts need Emphasis, Variability or Say It My Way to sound natural, for example sales demos with stressed product names.
4. You need more than 24 hours of generation a year, or transcription (48 hours on Business, none on Creator).

Business is $564 a year more than Creator at the yearly-billing prices above. Write down which of the four reasons you are paying for. If you cannot name one, Creator is the safer purchase.

**Choose Enterprise only if** you need several editors on one workspace, SSO, broadcasting rights or custom voice clones. Creator and Business each have a single editor seat.

## Who should not pay for Murf

- **One-off buyers.** Paying $228 up front for one explainer is a poor trade. Check whether monthly billing is offered at checkout, or use a tool with a small monthly plan.
- **Voice-cloning projects.** Self-serve Studio plans do not list voice cloning. Custom voice clones appear as an Enterprise add-on. If cloning a permitted voice is the brief, compare ElevenLabs first; our [ElevenLabs vs Murf comparison](/posts/elevenlabs-vs-murf-ai-2026/) covers the cloning tiers.
- **Developers.** Murf's API is billed separately by characters (the pricing page lists Falcon 2.1 at $0.01 per 1,000 characters on pay-as-you-go, checked October 31, 2026). A Studio subscription does not include API credit. See the [API plans and limits](https://help.murf.ai/murf-api-plans-and-limits) before you build.
- **Teams who need shared editing on a budget.** Sharing projects and access control are Enterprise features on the pricing table.

## How to test it yourself in 15 minutes

Use this schedule to judge Murf on your own script before paying. Have the script ready; account setup is not included.

1. **Minutes 0 to 3: pick the hard passage.** Choose 60 to 90 seconds you have the rights to use. Include a name, a number, a technical term and one emotional or emphasized line.
2. **Minutes 3 to 7: generate two voices.** Render the passage with two voices aimed at the same audience. Note the voice names and settings.
3. **Minutes 7 to 10: fix one problem.** Correct a mispronounced word with the pronunciation tools. Check the VGT counter before and after, so you know what a correction costs.
4. **Minutes 10 to 12: test delivery settings.** Change speed or pitch on the unchanged text and confirm the counter does not move, as the help center describes.
5. **Minutes 12 to 15: decide the plan.** If the voice passes, estimate your monthly VGT with the table above and choose Creator or Business. If it fails, stop and try another tool. Do not buy hoping the full script will sound better.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Murf AI free?

Murf offers a free trial with 10 minutes of voice generation, 10 minutes of transcription and no credit card, per its [trial guide](https://help.murf.ai/is-murf-free-to-use). You can preview and share projects, but downloads and commercial rights require a paid plan.

### How much does Murf AI cost per month?

On yearly billing, Creator is $19 a month ($228 billed annually) and Business is $66 a month ($792 billed annually), per the pricing page checked October 31, 2026. Enterprise is a custom quote. Monthly billing costs more; Murf says yearly saves 33%, so confirm the monthly price at checkout.

### Can I use Murf AI voices on YouTube?

Yes on paid plans. Murf's commercial rights article says paid Studio plans include commercial rights, including monetized YouTube content when the content is original. Follow YouTube's current rules on AI-generated audio.

### Does Murf Creator include a business license?

No. On the pricing table, the business license starts at Business. Murf recommends it for registered businesses, so check it before invoicing a corporate client on a Creator account.

### Can Murf clone my voice?

Not on the self-serve Creator or Business plans listed on the pricing page. Custom voice clones are an Enterprise add-on. Use any voice only with the speaker's permission.

### Is Murf better than ElevenLabs?

It depends on the job. Murf fits slide and Canva narration with a clear commercial-rights path. ElevenLabs has a lower monthly entry point and self-serve voice cloning. Our [head-to-head comparison](/posts/elevenlabs-vs-murf-ai-2026/) puts the plans side by side.

## Verdict: who should pay for Murf AI

Pay for **Murf Creator** if you narrate presentations or Canva videos most weeks, and your trial sample passes on the hardest part of your script. The $228 yearly cost works out to $19 a month for about 2 hours of generation a month, plus unlimited downloads and commercial rights.

Move to **Business** only for a concrete reason: a business license, PowerPoint or Slides, advanced voice controls, transcription or more yearly hours. Skip Murf if you need voice cloning, API access or a single short project.
