Murf Studio is a candidate for freelance voiceover work when you need to evaluate generated narration before committing to a plan. Start with your script and delivery requirements rather than an unsupported best-tool ranking.

## What can you do in the trial?

Murf's [trial-download help](https://help.murf.ai/can-i-download-my-project-during-the-free-trial) says downloads and exports require a paid plan. Use the trial to evaluate a representative script, not as a promise of a free final client deliverable. Check the [current plan page](https://murf.ai/pricing) for limits applicable to your account.

On October 5, the [Studio trial feature guide](https://help.murf.ai/is-murf-free-to-use) listed 10 minutes of voice generation, 10 minutes of transcription and no credit-card requirement. The [trial-duration FAQ](https://help.murf.ai/how-long-does-the-free-trial-last) describes a usage-limited trial with no fixed end date, rather than a monthly free allowance. Record the remaining counters in your account before testing revisions; these are documented limits, not an allowance we verified in a signed-in trial. Murf Dub and API have separate offers, so do not transfer their download or credit rules to Studio.

The [commercial-rights guidance](https://help.murf.ai/do-i-have-commercial-rights-over-the-voice-over-created) describes commercial rights on paid Studio plans and distinguishes business and broadcasting requirements. Match the license to your intended distribution, including any client use. This does not remove the need to have rights to the underlying script or other assets.

## Define the output before choosing a plan

Write down the project's language, length, pronunciation requirements, file format and delivery date. Clarify whether your client needs a one-off narration or regular revisions. Those requirements determine what you should test.

| Requirement | Question to resolve |
|---|---|
| Voice selection | Does the preview fit this audience and subject? |
| Pronunciation | Can names and specialist terms be corrected? |
| Pacing | Can the sample fit the intended section length? |
| Exports | Is the required download included in the selected plan? |
| Licensing | Does the client's use fit the applicable permissions? |
| Team workflow | Who must edit, review and approve the project? |
| Budget | What are the payment term, renewal and revision limits? |

## Run a small sample evaluation

Use a script that resembles the real project rather than a generic promotional sentence. Include a difficult name, a date or number, a technical term and a transition. Listen for errors and document the effort needed to correct them.

Keep the script, chosen settings and date. Compare the same sample with a second tool if necessary. A clear record can establish which workflow fits your project; this article does not invent a winner in the absence of such a test.

## Calculate the commitment

Check whether the displayed monthly amount requires annual billing. Compare the total amount due, included generation, export rights and potential changes to your workload. A small headline price is not automatically a suitable client-production budget.

Include revision rounds in the estimate. A change to the script after approval can require another generation and another review. Agree with your client on how corrections and new requests are handled before you produce the complete project.

## Murf pricing: budget for revisions, not just final audio

Studio uses Voice Generation Time (VGT). According to the [official VGT explanation](https://help.murf.ai/what-is-voice-generation-time-vgt), generating new text or changing text consumes the duration of the affected sub-block. The final delivered recording can therefore be shorter than the total generation you paid for.

For example, a five-minute first version followed by a regenerated one-minute section would require six minutes of generation if those are the actual rendered durations. This is an illustrative budget, not a measured Murf benchmark. Record the usage counter before and after your sample rather than assuming every script edit costs the full project length.

The [voice-settings guidance](https://help.murf.ai/does-changing-voice-settings-sped-pitch-etc-consume-vgt) distinguishes adjustments to already rendered, unchanged text from text edits: settings such as speed and pitch do not consume additional VGT in that case. Keep client script approval separate from voice-settings approval.

Unused VGT is not an unlimited savings balance. The [carry-forward policy](https://help.murf.ai/vgt-carry-forward) limits carried credit to the following billing cycle and describes loss on cancellation. Confirm the current terms before relying on a remaining balance for later projects.

### Is API pricing the same as Studio pricing?

No: Murf documents [API plans and limits](https://help.murf.ai/murf-api-plans-and-limits) separately, using character allowances and request limits. An API character price is not the price of a Studio subscription or a promise of a particular audio duration. Choose the product first, then check its billing unit, export requirements and permissions. This review evaluates the Studio buying decision, not an implemented API workflow.

## Complete the delivery process

1. Confirm rights to the script and supporting assets.
2. Agree on the sample, voice and pronunciation list.
3. Verify that the selected plan supports export and intended use.
4. Produce and review the accepted sections.
5. Check the final file against the client's delivery requirements.
6. Retain the approved version and necessary revision notes.

A generated voice does not establish a person's endorsement. Resolve permission separately when a project involves an identifiable person or supplied voice material.
