GetResponse and Mailchimp can both appear on an email-platform shortlist. For a freelancer or small business, the decision should start with a defined subscriber journey, required integrations and a complete cost quote.

**Scope:** this is a documentation-based comparison checked on 5 October 2026. We have not run a controlled account comparison, inbox-placement benchmark or conversion study. We do not name an overall winner or assign product scores.

## Compare the plan you would actually buy

| Requirement | GetResponse documentation | Mailchimp documentation | Your decision evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial access | Pricing page advertises a 14-day trial | Pricing page lists trial and Free options | Check eligibility, renewal and limits in your own flow |
| Automation | Starter lists one custom workflow; Marketer lists unlimited workflows | Capabilities depend on the selected plan | Map the same expected journey and confirm plan access |
| Webinars | Listed under Creator on the pricing page | Not established by this comparison | Check your required webinar system and integration separately |
| Contact and send limits | Obtain the quote for your list and plan | Free is listed with a 250-contact limit; pricing warns about paid-plan overages | Record contacts, sends and over-limit consequences |
| Total project cost | Quote required | Quote required | Include integrations, setup and maintenance |

Sources: [GetResponse pricing and plan comparison](https://www.getresponse.com/pricing), [Mailchimp marketing pricing](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/). These are dated documentation observations, not account tests. Offers, taxes and plan limits can change.

Do not treat a free trial as a permanent free plan. Read the exact trial conditions before adding payment information. A feature appearing somewhere in a product family does not mean it is included in the cheapest plan.

## Build a comparable cost sheet

Use the same contact count, monthly email sends, currency and billing interval for both quotes. Record which contacts count toward billing, what happens when limits are exceeded, what an introductory offer becomes at renewal and how cancellation works. Do not compare one platform's annual promotional rate with another's monthly regular rate.

Add the work needed around the subscription: migrating fields, rebuilding forms, connecting a booking or store system, checking domain setup, producing content and maintaining automations. If webinars are essential, include the required plan or separate webinar tool. Only count a replaced subscription as savings after confirming that you can retire it without losing required functionality.

## Run the same permitted pilot

Choose a small journey that reflects your real project: signup, any confirmation, delivery of a promised resource, a useful follow-up and unsubscribe. Use permitted test addresses and document expected versus observed behavior. Do not buy or scrape a list to make the pilot look larger.

Test your required integration with a clearly identified sample record. Check whether field mapping, subscription status and suppression behave as intended. A vendor integration logo does not prove your specific workflow works.

Measure operator time for setup, revisions and routine maintenance. If neither platform fits the hours you can sustain, simplify the journey before adding features.

## Deliverability remains unmeasured here

An email arriving at one test address is not an inbox-placement benchmark. We have no comparable dataset that establishes a deliverability winner or tie. Record the setup requirements from each provider's current documentation and keep any observed delivery results separate from broad performance claims.

## Check the exit and client ownership

Use the [newsletter client handoff checklist](/posts/newsletter-platform-exit-checklist/) for a five-step export and acceptance process. Record unresolved tasks before deciding the migration is complete.

Agree who owns the account, sending domain, list and content. Identify required export fields and any content or workflow that would need rebuilding when leaving. An export button is not proof of a complete migration. Keep permission records and personal subscriber data private.

## Decision

Choose the candidate that passes your essential requirements with an acceptable verified bill and measured workload. Defer if cost, ownership, suppression or an essential integration remains unresolved. This comparison does not establish that GetResponse is cheaper for every list or that Mailchimp is easier for every operator.
