## Who should choose which plan?

**Choose Basic if you are still defining your process.** Make a clean chart, mark the levels you intend to revisit and write down your review routine. A paid subscription is premature when you cannot identify the specific task it enables.

**Choose Essential for a compact, repeatable setup.** My recommendation is to start here when Basic interrupts an established routine. Keep a written list of the instruments and conditions you actually monitor. Remove abandoned alerts before deciding that you need a larger allowance.

**Choose Plus for a broader active watchlist.** As a planning example, monitoring 12 pairs with two separate technical conditions each requires 24 technical alerts. That is workload arithmetic, not a measured trading result. Count price alerts separately and leave room for temporary setups.

**Choose Premium when you can explain the additional workspace.** A reasonable reason is a review process that needs several simultaneous views and frequent switching is causing missed checks. Buying it because it sounds more serious is a weak reason. Sketch the workspace first and label the purpose of every pane.

**Consider Ultimate only after checking eligibility and capacity.** Most readers should establish why the lower options cannot do the job before committing to the largest bill. Professional classification also needs checking independently of how actively you trade.

**Who should not pay yet?** Anyone expecting a chart subscription to supply a profitable strategy, repair a mismatched exchange symbol or remove the need to verify orders. Those are separate problems. A better workspace can improve organization without establishing better trading results.

## Alerts: build the routine before expanding it

## Crypto automation: a webhook is only part of the system

A webhook sends an HTTP POST notification to an external application. TradingView requires two-factor authentication, accepts ports 80 and 443, and cancels a request if the server takes more than three seconds to process it. Delivery can fail. Source: [TradingView webhook configuration documentation](https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000529348-how-to-configure-webhook-alerts/?aff_id=1173587), checked November 13, 2026.

Before buying a plan for automation, map the receiver, any bot service and the exchange connection. Budget those separately. Ask how the receiver handles duplicate messages, rejected orders and an unavailable exchange. Keep credentials out of alert messages and evaluate the route in a demo or paper environment first.

## FAQ

### Is TradingView free for crypto traders?

Basic is free and is a sensible starting point for a simple chart routine. Upgrade when a documented limit blocks a task you already perform.

### Which TradingView plan should a crypto beginner choose?

Start with Basic. Essential is my first paid shortlist once your workflow needs more capacity; a beginner does not need the largest tier merely to learn charting.

### Do I need paid market data for crypto charts?

Check the exact symbol. TradingView's documentation says its listed cryptocurrency exchanges provide real-time data. Separate stock or futures data subscriptions should not be confused with the chart plan.

### Is TradingView better than Kraken Pro?

Choose by workflow. Kraken Pro may cover your existing exchange-chart needs; TradingView is worth considering when a dedicated chart and alert workspace solves a specific recurring problem.

### Can TradingView alerts automatically trade crypto?

A webhook can notify another application, but that application needs an execution integration. Verify the entire route and failure handling before using live funds; the alert alone does not establish order execution.

### Do crypto traders need Premium?

Only when its added capacity or features solve an identified requirement. Count your active conditions and sketch your workspace before committing to a larger annual bill.
