HubSpot is worth evaluating when a client needs a shared record of leads, conversations and deals. Before moving a spreadsheet into it, check whether the required workflow fits the chosen product edition and the client's ongoing budget.

This is a source-based buying guide, updated October 8, 2026. It is not a hands-on benchmark, an AI accuracy test or a claim that one CRM beats every alternative. The sample acceptance test below is a proposed exercise, not a completed customer deployment.

## Start with the actual free limits

HubSpot's [official CRM page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) currently describes its free CRM as supporting **up to two users and 1,000 contacts**, with no expiration date. Our previous statement that this tier had no contact limit was incorrect.

Do not assume a limit for one product or edition applies to every HubSpot subscription. Ask the client how many people must edit records and how many contacts will be imported before choosing a plan.

## Choose the job before the bundle

Write one required outcome for each part of the project. For example, “a new enquiry has an owner and a next-action date” is a useful acceptance criterion. “We need automation” is too broad to price or test.

| Client job | Question to resolve before purchase | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Lead intake | Which fields are required and who owns the record? | A sample form and resulting record |
| Sales follow-up | Which actions are manual and which must be automated? | A demonstrated workflow in the selected edition |
| Marketing email | Which contacts will be eligible to receive campaigns? | Contact classification and the quoted contact tier |
| Customer support | Who can see and update a customer issue? | A permission test using separate roles |
| Reporting | Which decision should the report support? | A reconciled example with known inputs |
| AI assistance | What requires human approval before reaching a customer? | A reviewed sample and escalation rule |

Do not present a demo in a higher tier as proof that the client's chosen tier includes the same feature. Record the product, edition and access conditions beside each requirement.

## Price the subscription the client will actually use

HubSpot's [Marketing Hub pricing page](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing) separates seat, marketing-contact and onboarding considerations. Its FAQ also explains that marketing-contact tiers and contract renewal affect downgrade choices. Read the current quote and contract conditions before committing to a contact tier.

A Marketing Hub price is not a universal price for every HubSpot product. Instead of reusing one Free/Starter/Professional/Enterprise table for the whole platform, request a quote tied to the exact products and usage required.

Use this original cost worksheet:

| Cost item | What to write down |
|---|---|
| Product and edition | Exact subscription name for every required workflow |
| Users and permissions | Paid editing seats, viewing access and role requirements |
| Contact usage | Imported contacts, marketing-eligible contacts and expected growth |
| Setup | Import cleanup, field mapping, duplicate handling and training |
| Required services | Onboarding and implementation charges in the quote |
| AI or usage allowances | Included allowance, overage handling and approval owner |
| Contract | Billing term, renewal date and downgrade/cancellation conditions |
| Ongoing operation | Monthly review time, exception handling and data maintenance |

Compare total delivery cost with the [freelance tool project-cost worksheet](/posts/freelance-tool-project-cost/). A subscription can fit the budget while the cleanup and support workload still makes the project unprofitable.

## A small CRM acceptance test

Use fictitious data first. Do not upload a real client contact list just to explore the interface.

1. Create five clearly labelled sample leads, including one duplicate and one missing a required field.
2. Assign an owner and a next-action date to each valid lead.
3. Move one lead through the proposed stages and check whether its history remains understandable.
4. Confirm the duplicate and incomplete record handling agreed with the client.
5. Test a staff role and a restricted role separately; document what each can see and change.
6. Reconcile a simple report against the five known sample records.
7. Demonstrate the export and handoff process the client will need, including anything the export does not preserve.

Record pass/fail, the edition tested and any manual workaround. This exercise does not establish conversion improvement, email deliverability or AI accuracy. Those need separate evidence from the real workflow.

## Keep the client in control

Agree who owns the account, billing, domain connections and exported data before implementation. The client should be able to understand what happens when your engagement ends.

A useful handoff includes the field dictionary, workflow diagram, list of connected services, permission owners, renewal dates and a short recovery procedure. Avoid making a contractor's personal inbox the only way to recover the project.

If AI drafts a message or suggests an action, identify who checks facts and approves the output. A connected CRM record is not evidence that every generated statement is correct.

## When to keep the setup smaller

For a solo operator with a small lead list, first test whether the free allowance and a manual follow-up routine meet the actual need. For a multi-person client, resolve access and ownership before promising that a free setup will be sufficient.

For an email-focused project, compare the required subscriber journey with the [Kit checklist](/posts/kit-review-2026/) and [Systeme.io funnel checklist](/posts/systeme-io-review-2026/). These guides address different jobs; they are not evidence that their plans are interchangeable with a CRM.
